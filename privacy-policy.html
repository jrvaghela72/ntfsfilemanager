1. Introduction & scope

This Privacy Policy explains, in specific and complete terms, how the NTFS File Manager Android application (the "App," "we," "us," or "the Developer") handles information when you download, install, and use it. It is written to satisfy the disclosure requirements of the Google Play Developer Program Policies — including the User Data policy and the Permissions policy for sensitive permissions such as All Files Access and Query All Packages — and to give you an accurate, complete account of the App's data practices rather than a generic template.

This Policy covers the App itself. It does not cover: (a) the operating-system-level services described in Section 5, which Google, not the Developer, operates and controls; (b) the cloud storage providers described in Section 4, which you connect and control, and which operate under their own privacy policies once a file reaches them; (c) third-party apps you choose to open a file with, or destinations you navigate to via links in the App (see Section 14); or (d) any website, repository, or service not operated by the Developer. By installing or using the App, you acknowledge that you have read and understood this Policy.

2. Information we do not collect

The App is primarily an offline, local file-management utility. It reads, writes, copies, moves, renames, deletes, compresses, and displays files that already exist on your device's internal storage or on a connected USB (OTG) drive. The one feature that involves the network at all is Cloud storage — connecting your own Google Drive, Dropbox, Microsoft OneDrive, Box, or pCloud account, entirely at your initiative — and Section 4 describes exactly what that does and does not involve. Outside of that one, opt-in feature, the App does not have, and cannot use, any of the following:

  • An account system, registration, login, or user profile operated by the Developer. (Signing in to a cloud storage account you already have is between you and that provider — see Section 4.)
  • A backend server, cloud storage, or sync service operated by the Developer. Nothing you do in the App — including a cloud file transfer — passes through any server the Developer runs, because no such server exists.
  • Advertising SDKs, advertising identifiers, or ad-tracking technology of any kind.
  • Analytics or telemetry SDKs (e.g., no Firebase Analytics, no third-party crash-reporting SDKs, no session-replay or behavioral-tracking libraries) bundled by the Developer.
  • Access to your contacts, call logs, SMS/MMS messages, calendar, precise or coarse location, camera, microphone, or biometric sensors. The App neither requests nor uses any permission covering these categories.
  • Any mechanism to read the contents, filenames, metadata, or existence of your files and transmit them anywhere the Developer can see, store, or access. A file you explicitly copy to a cloud account goes straight from your device to that provider, as described in Section 4 — the Developer is not in that path and neither sees nor retains a copy.

3. Permissions we request, and exactly why

Every permission the App declares is a permission it actually uses for a specific, user-visible file-management function. None of them are used to collect information about you, and each is requested only at the point it is actually needed — not in a bundle at first launch.

Storage access — All Files Access (Android 11+) / legacy storage permissions (Android 12 and earlier)

File management is the App's entire purpose, so direct access to your device's shared storage (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE on Android 11 and later, or the legacy READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE/WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE permissions on Android 12 and earlier) is the one permission the App genuinely cannot function without. Prominent in-app disclosure: before the system's All Files Access settings screen is ever shown, the App first displays its own explanation screen stating exactly why the permission is required and what will not work without it; you must take an explicit action on that screen before the system permission flow opens. The App uses this permission exclusively to let you browse, open, copy, move, rename, delete, compress, and otherwise manage files you choose, on storage you choose. It is never used to scan, catalogue, index, upload, or transmit your files or file listings to the Developer or to any third party.

Media permissions (Android 13+) — READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, READ_MEDIA_AUDIO

These granular media permissions supplement All Files Access on newer Android versions so the App can generate thumbnails and read metadata (such as image dimensions or audio/video duration) for the Photos, Videos, and Music quick-category views and the file-properties panel. They are used solely to render those files' previews and details inside the App on your screen.

USB device access

Requested only when you physically connect a USB (OTG) drive, through Android's standard USB-host permission dialog, so the App can communicate with that specific drive directly (to mount, read, and write NTFS or exFAT volumes). This access is scoped to the device you approve and is not requested speculatively.

Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, Android 13+)

Used only to show and let you control the progress of long-running operations — file transfers, compression, and drive formatting — including while you are outside the App, and to let you pause, cancel, or resume them. Requested the first time such an operation is about to run, not at install.

Foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC)

Lets a file transfer, compression, or format operation you started keep running with a visible, ongoing notification if you navigate away from the App, rather than being silently killed by the operating system. This does not run in the background without your knowledge — the persistent notification required by Android for this permission is always visible while it is active.

Query all installed apps (QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES)

The App's Apps feature lets you view, launch, back up (extract as an APK), and manage the applications installed on your device — a file-management capability the Google Play Permissions policy specifically recognizes as an approved use of this permission for file-manager apps that work with the widest array of file types, including APKs. This permission only lets the App read the on-device list of installed packages (package name, label, icon, version) so it can display them to you; that list is never transmitted anywhere or logged by the Developer.

Internet (INTERNET)

Two things use this permission. First, your device reaching Google Play's own servers for the two Play platform features described in Section 5 (in-app update checks and the in-app review prompt) — this happens automatically and does not involve your files. Second, the optional Cloud storage feature described in Section 4: if, and only if, you explicitly connect a cloud account, the App talks directly to that provider's own servers (never a server the Developer operates) to sign you in and to carry out the specific file transfers you request. If you never connect a cloud account, the App makes no network request of that kind at all.

4. Cloud storage accounts you connect

The App's Cloud section lets you connect your own Google Drive, Dropbox, Microsoft OneDrive, Box, or pCloud account so you can browse it and copy files to and from your device, the same way you already do with Internal Storage or a USB drive. This is entirely opt-in — nothing in this section happens unless you deliberately tap "Add cloud account" and complete that provider's own sign-in.

How sign-in works

Connecting an account hands you off to that provider's own sign-in screen (Google Drive uses Android's system Credential Manager; Dropbox, Box, and pCloud use a browser tab; Microsoft OneDrive uses Microsoft's own authentication library) — you enter your credentials directly with the provider, never with the App. The App receives back only a limited-scope access token proving you approved the connection, never your password. For Google Drive specifically, the App requests the narrowest available scope (drive.file), which only ever sees files the App itself created or that you explicitly add — not your whole Drive.

What the App stores, and where

The access token (and, where the provider issues one, a refresh token) for each connected account is stored in EncryptedSharedPreferences, encrypted at rest using a key held in your device's Android Keystore — the same mechanism a password manager relies on. This stays on your device; it is never sent to the Developer, because the Developer has no server to send it to. Disconnecting an account (or uninstalling the App) removes it immediately.

What happens when you copy a file

When you copy or move a file between your device and a connected cloud account, that file's bytes travel directly between your device and the provider's own servers, over an encrypted connection, using the token described above. The Developer's infrastructure is not part of that path in any way — there is no server of the Developer's for the data to pass through, and the Developer cannot see, log, or retain the file, its name, or its contents. Once a file lands on the provider's side, or on your device, it is subject to that provider's own storage and privacy practices, not this Policy: Google (Drive), Dropbox, Microsoft (OneDrive), Box, and pCloud.

Your control over a connected account

You can disconnect any cloud account at any time from the App's Cloud section, which immediately deletes its stored token from your device and stops the App from accessing that account. Disconnecting in the App does not delete anything from the provider's side, and does not revoke the App's access from the provider's end in every case — as an extra step, you can also review and revoke the App's access directly from your account settings on the provider's own website (for example, Google Account → Security → Third-party access) at any time.

5. Data handled by Google Play system services

The App integrates three Google Play platform features. Each is Google's own infrastructure, operating under Google's Privacy Policy — the Developer does not receive, store, or process the data these features handle, and cannot configure them beyond what Google Play provides by default:

  • Play In-App Updates — checks whether a newer version of the App is available on Google Play and can download it in the background so you can install it without leaving the App. This involves your device contacting Google Play's servers; it does not involve any data from your files or file activity.
  • Play In-App Review — after clear, successful use of the App, may show Google's own built-in review prompt. The Developer never learns whether you were shown the prompt, dismissed it, or left a rating.
  • Android Vitals (automatic crash & ANR reporting) — once the App is published, Google Play automatically collects basic crash and "Application Not Responding" diagnostics (such as stack traces, device model, and OS version) for every app distributed on Play, to help identify stability problems. This is a standard Google Play platform feature, not something the Developer adds, configures, or receives raw access to beyond the aggregated diagnostics Google Play Console exposes to all developers for their own apps.

None of these three features are used for advertising, cross-app tracking, or building a profile of your file activity.

6. Data the app creates and stores on your device

To provide certain features, the App stores small amounts of data locally, entirely within its own private app storage or within folders you control on your device. None of it is transmitted anywhere; all of it stays under your control and is removed when you clear the App's data or uninstall it (except where noted):

  • App preferences (e.g., Show Hidden Files, Show Safe Folder, USB transfer buffer size, and your chosen App Language) — stored in a local, private preferences file on your device or, for App Language on Android 13 and later, via Android's own per-app language system setting. Either way, the choice stays on your device and is used only to display the App's own interface in that language.
  • Bookmarks — the internal-storage file/folder paths you choose to bookmark, stored locally in a private preferences file. Never available for OTG/USB paths.
  • Trash — for internal storage only, deleting an item moves it to a hidden .NtfsHelperTrash folder on your own device storage rather than erasing it immediately, together with a small metadata record of its original location and deletion time, so it can be restored. Items are automatically and permanently purged from Trash after 7 days, or sooner if you empty it yourself. USB (OTG) deletions are immediate and permanent and never pass through Trash, consistent with how removable drives are conventionally treated.
  • Thumbnail cache — small in-memory previews generated from your own images and videos to speed up browsing; not written to a persistent file and cleared when the App is closed or memory is reclaimed.
  • Safe Folder — an optional feature that hides a folder behind your device's own lock-screen credential (PIN, pattern, password, or biometric). The App never receives, stores, processes, or has any access to your credential itself; that confirmation is handled entirely by Android's own system security APIs, the same mechanism used to unlock your device. Safe Folder restricts casual access to a folder — it is not encryption, and files inside it are stored on disk the same way as any other file on your device.
  • Cloud account sign-in tokens — if you connect a Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, Box, or pCloud account (Section 4), the resulting access/refresh token is stored in EncryptedSharedPreferences backed by your device's Android Keystore. It never leaves your device and is deleted immediately when you disconnect that account or uninstall the App.

7. How information is used

Because the App does not collect personal information, it does not use, process, profile, analyze, or monetize any personal information — there is none for it to act on. The on-device data described in Section 6 is used exclusively to provide the specific feature it belongs to (e.g., Bookmarks are used only to show your bookmarked items; Trash metadata is used only to support restore and auto-expiry).

8. Data sharing, sale, and disclosure

The Developer does not sell, rent, trade, or otherwise monetize any personal information — because none is collected, there is none to sell. Nor does the Developer share personal information with third parties for their own marketing or advertising purposes. The data flows outside your device are: (a) the Google Play platform features described in Section 5, which every app distributed on Google Play relies on at the platform level and which are not initiated, controlled, or monetized by the Developer; and (b) if, and only if, you choose to connect a cloud storage account, the direct device-to-provider file transfers described in Section 4, which you initiate and control, and which the Developer is not a party to and cannot see. Neither is a disclosure of your data by the Developer to a third party, since the Developer is never in possession of it to begin with.

The Developer may disclose information if legally required to do so (for example, in response to a valid legal process) — however, as the App collects no personal information in the first place, the Developer holds none to disclose in practice, including whether any particular install has connected a cloud account, which provider, or any file activity through it — none of that is transmitted to or logged by the Developer.

9. Data security

Your files and app data are protected by Android's standard application sandboxing and filesystem permission model, which isolates the App's own private storage from other apps on your device. Any network communication the App triggers is limited to the Google Play platform features in Section 5 and, if you've connected one, the cloud provider described in Section 4 — both use encrypted (HTTPS/TLS) connections. Cloud sign-in tokens are additionally encrypted at rest via Android Keystore, as described in Sections 4 and 6. As noted in Section 6, Safe Folder restricts access via your device credential but does not encrypt files — if you require encryption-at-rest for especially sensitive files, use a dedicated encryption tool in addition to Safe Folder. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure, and the Developer cannot guarantee absolute security, but no personal data leaving your device other than through a cloud connection you explicitly set up yourself is, by design, the App's core security posture.

10. Data retention and deletion

The Developer operates no server and therefore retains no personal data of yours anywhere off your device. On-device data is retained for as long as it is useful to you and is fully within your control: uninstalling the App removes its preferences, bookmarks, and cache; emptying Trash (or waiting out the 7-day auto-expiry) permanently removes trashed items; disabling Safe Folder removes its gating (the folder and its contents remain, simply no longer hidden behind the credential prompt); disconnecting a cloud account, or uninstalling the App, immediately deletes its stored sign-in token from your device (Section 4). Files you've already copied to a cloud account, or already copied to your device from one, are retained wherever they now live, under that storage location's own retention rules — the App has no further involvement once a transfer completes.
